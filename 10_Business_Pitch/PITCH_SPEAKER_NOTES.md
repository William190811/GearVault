# GearVault — Pitch Speaker Notes (Markdown backup)

Full formatted version: [GearVault_Pitch_Script.docx](GearVault_Pitch_Script.docx). This file is a plain-text backup of the same script so it's easy to diff/edit outside Word, plus rehearsal cues.

Total runtime target: **3.5–4 minutes spoken**, inside the assignment's 3–5 minute window. Split evenly across 3 speakers.

## Part 1 — The Problem (Partner 1, ~60–75 sec)

> Quick question: how many of you have earbuds sitting loose at the bottom of your backpack right now, tangled up with a USB drive, maybe a charging cable? Raise your hand.
>
> Yeah. Same here.
>
> Every day, students, teachers, anyone hauling a laptop bag around deals with this. You toss your earbuds in next to your textbooks and your water bottle, and by the time you actually need them, the case is scratched, the cable's a knot, and you're digging around the bottom of the bag for a USB drive you swear you packed.
>
> Backpacks are built to carry books. Nobody designed them to protect the small stuff — the stuff you actually reach for every day.
>
> So we built something that does.

**Cue:** pause 2–3 seconds after the opening question — let hands actually go up before continuing.

## Part 2 — The Product (Partner 2, ~60–90 sec)

> This is GearVault. It's a small hard-shell case, 3D-printed, that lives inside your backpack — a dedicated vault for your earbuds, USB drives, SD cards, whatever small stuff you don't want scratched up.
>
> *[HOLD UP THE PRODUCT HERE]*
>
> Open it up and you've got an earbud pocket, slots sized to hold your USB drives snugly, and right in the middle, this raised post. That's our cable-wrap post — wind your cable around it before you close the lid, so it can't tangle with anything else in there. We haven't seen anything else this small do that.
>
> The lid snaps shut on its own built-in hinge. No glue, no screws, nothing extra. It just clicks.
>
> *[DEMO: SNAP IT SHUT]*
>
> We made two sizes. GearVault Mini holds your earbuds and a couple of drives, good if you travel light. GearVault Pro is bigger — room for four USB drives plus a dongle tray, for anyone carrying a full laptop bag.
>
> We print every one of these ourselves, by hand.

**Cue:** this is the demo beat — actually hold up a Mini and a Pro, and audibly snap the lid on "click." This section sells the product more than any line of the script does.

## Part 3 — Cost, Sales & the Ask (Partner 3, ~60–90 sec)

> Here's the pricing. GearVault Mini is Rp25,000. GearVault Pro is Rp35,000. That's about what you'd pay for a coffee and a snack, for something that protects the gear you use every day.
>
> We're already selling these to classmates, teachers, and staff around campus, and every sale teaches us something. We're collecting feedback from every customer we sell to, and we're already sketching out a version two based on what people actually tell us.
>
> So here's the ask. If you carry a backpack with a laptop, earbuds, or a USB drive you'd rather not see scratched up, GearVault already solves that, today, for less than a snack.
>
> We've got Minis and Pros with us right now. Who wants to be one of our first ten customers?

**Cue:** have the [Sales Tracker](../05_Sales_Evidence/SALES_TRACKER.md) ready to log a buyer's name on the spot if someone says yes right after the pitch.

## General delivery notes
- Make eye contact with the room, not your notes.
- Keep handoffs between speakers quick — no "okay, now I'll talk about...", just start your part.
- Rehearse with a timer. If you're creeping toward 5 minutes, trim lines rather than talk faster.
- Swap the "Partner 1/2/3" labels above for real names once assigned — do a find/replace here and in the docx.
